Proposed wording for the call

One workflow, two research traditions

The joint DocAgents and AgentDM workshop at COLING 2027 invites work on language and multimodal agents that interpret documents, analyse structured data, and carry out tasks whose results people can check. DocAgents brings research on how agents read pages, interpret layout and visual evidence, and act on information distributed across documents. AgentDM brings research on how agents turn a natural-language goal into an executable analysis over tables, databases, graphs, time series, or spatiotemporal records. Both communities ask how an agent decides what to do next, checks the result of an action, and explains what warrants its conclusion.

01

A motivating example

Consider an analyst asking why a reported increase in demand appears in one region but not another. A document agent might trace the claim from a chart to a footnote that changes the definition of demand. A data-mining agent might query the underlying records, align the reporting periods, and test whether the regional difference persists.

The conclusion depends on both reading the qualification correctly and performing a valid comparison. A system that exposes the footnote, query, and calculation gives the analyst a way to check each step.

We welcome contributions to either research area and to their intersection. A study of evidence localisation in degraded documents, an agent that designs forecasting experiments on time series, and a system that reconciles reports with database records are all within scope. Contributions may address an individual research problem or an end-to-end workflow.

Topics of interest

From source evidence to defensible decisions

We welcome focused contributions within either community as well as systems that connect documents and structured data.

D

Research track 01

Document intelligence and grounded reasoning

Document extraction and multimodal, multi-page understanding

Reading text, layout, tables, charts, and images together. Topics include OCR-based and OCR-free extraction of text, entities, key-value pairs, and tables while preserving links to source evidence. It includes following references between pages in long documents, linking captions and footnotes to the right visual elements and recovering the structure of complex or noisy documents.

Evidence selection and attribution

Retrieving the pages or regions needed for a multi-hop question, citing evidence at the level of a passage, table cell, or figure, and checking whether it supports the extracted information and the answer. We welcome work on confidence estimation and on asking a human expert to resolve ambiguity.

Multilingual, low-resource, and specialised documents

Methods and resources for rare scripts, endangered languages, historical archives, and technical documents. Relevant questions include how errors propagate from recognition and extraction to downstream reasoning and how well a method transfers across scripts, layouts, and domains.

A

Research track 02

Autonomous mining of structured data

Grounding goals and planning analyses

Mapping a user’s request to schemas, entities, time windows, and executable operations. Topics include semantic parsing, task decomposition, model selection, workflow synthesis, and agents that revise a plan in light of intermediate results.

Discovery, prediction, and decision support

Agents that formulate and test hypotheses, mine relational patterns, detect anomalies, forecast changes, or design experiments. We welcome studies on tables and databases as well as graphs, time series, and spatiotemporal data, including how agents choose suitable comparisons and validate discoveries.

Analytical tools, memory, and collaboration

Integrating SQL, Python, databases, retrieval systems, and statistical or predictive models. Topics include maintaining state across an analysis, coordinating specialised agents, and allowing an analyst to inspect, correct, or resume a workflow.

Shared research questions

Where the communities meet

01

Linking evidence across sources

How can an agent connect a statement in a report to the correct entity, unit, date, and record in a database? We invite work on preserving provenance when extracted information becomes structured records, reconciling definitions, and handling conflicting or missing evidence.

02

Tool choice, verification, and recovery

When should an agent read another page, query a database, rerun a calculation, or ask for clarification? We welcome methods for checking intermediate actions, revising failed plans, and recording enough provenance for another person to reproduce the result.

03

Evaluation of the full workflow

Datasets, benchmarks, simulators, and error analyses that distinguish perception, extraction, or retrieval failures from planning, execution, and interpretation errors. We welcome realistic, noisy, multi-page document collections. Measures of interest include task success, extraction accuracy, citation accuracy, numerical and statistical validity, calibration, and generalisation under distribution shift.

04

Responsible and efficient deployment

Privacy-preserving extraction and analysis, fairness and bias analysis, human oversight, audit trails, and regulatory compliance. We welcome deployment studies that report accuracy alongside latency, cost, and scale, as well as negative results from settings such as finance, healthcare, law, science, education, and urban systems.

Types of contribution

Many forms of useful evidence

We invite methods and systems papers, datasets and evaluation protocols, empirical and reproducibility studies, deployment reports, analyses of failures or negative results, and position papers that formulate concrete research questions for either community.

Planning call

Decisions for the organizers

Internal working notes
01

First call and submission details

Each team could review its specialist topics and nominate one organizer to edit the common scope. The timetable circulated by DocAgents lists 21 November 2026 for the first call, 21 December for the second call, and 5 January 2027 for direct submissions.

For the public CFP, confirm the submission link, deadline time zone, paper categories and lengths, archival options, and the ARR commitment deadline. The DocAgents proposal envisages both direct submissions and ARR commitments through OpenReview. The two teams should agree on these routes for the joint workshop.

02

One-day programme

One option is to open with two complementary invited perspectives, one on grounding decisions in document evidence and one on autonomous discovery over structured data. A morning session on methods and evaluation could mix papers from both communities. Afternoon spotlights and posters could focus on systems, applications, and lessons from deployment.

When an agent’s conclusion is challenged, what should a document reader, a data analyst, or an end user be able to inspect or rerun?

Both teams could reconfirm speaker availability for COLING before announcing names or allocating slots.

03

Reviewing and division of work

Both proposals plan for three reviews per submission and a maximum load of three papers per reviewer. The two teams could pool their reviewer lists, identify gaps in coverage, and assign cross-cutting papers to reviewers with complementary expertise.

On the call, we could also identify reusable website or mailing-list materials and agree who will maintain the joint CFP and website, configure OpenReview, coordinate invitations, and circulate the call to each community.
